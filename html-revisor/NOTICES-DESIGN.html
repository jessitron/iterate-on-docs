Notices: how you see what the agent changed

A notice is a mark on an element whose content changed since you last saw it, and a count in the docket. This page holds what a notice is, which element wears one, what takes one off, and the open design for removals and moves. DESIGN.html draws how a notice looks, with the real stylesheet. NAVIGATION-DESIGN.html is moving through notices by key. CONCURRENCY.html is the plan this is part of.

What do you call the agent's change reaching you? A notice: green crop marks on the element, a check tile beside it, and a count in the docket. The agent hears of your changes another way, through an Announcement. The words you see say “change”; “notice” is the code's word. ✓
Which element wears the mark? The innermost one whose own content changed: a reworded paragraph marks the paragraph and not its section. A new element wears one mark around the whole of it. Where a change shows has every shape. ✓
Where does the ack sit? Beside the directive's marker, near where the directive box opens: the check tile. The docket has a check for all of them. ✓
What does a check take off? Its element's mark and every mark inside it. Flipping through the changes shows the inner ones first, then the one around them. ✓
What does leaving a directive do to a mark? It takes off the mark on that element and on each element around it, and not the marks inside it. You wrote on it, so you saw it. A new element a cleared mark covered counts as seen with it, so no mark appears where none showed. ✓
What does your own edit do? What your change made counts as seen. Your edit on a marked element takes its mark off. ✓

What counts as seen

The server keeps, per page, each element's content as you last saw it. Content that differs wears a notice.

When What the server counts as seen by you
You open the page for the first time since the server started The whole page. No marks.
You save content in the directive box, or change a form field What your change made. Your edit on a marked scope takes its mark off.
You click a check That element and everything inside it. The docket's check: the whole page.
You leave a directive The element you wrote on and each element around it, and not the marks inside it. Clearing a directive doesn't count.
You open the directive box and close it Nothing new. The mark stays.
You reload Nothing new. The marks are still there.
The server restarts It forgets: the next open counts the whole page as seen.

Where a change shows

The problem: a change can happen outside any one element, and then the mark it gets can be far bigger than the change.

The rule today

A mark goes on an element with an ID. Every scope has one: what you can pick, leave a directive on, and edit as markdown. A few elements that are not scopes have one too: an hr, a table row, each element of an svg, and a form field. The code calls any element with an ID a unit, and so does this page; a question below asks whether that word should stand. Each unit's own content is its tag, its attributes, its text, and the order of the units in it. When that differs from what you saw, the unit wears a mark. An element without an ID, such as a strong, belongs to the unit around it.

The agent's command (replace, add, remove, move) takes any unit.

New content is always one element or a run of them: add takes HTML, and replace can bring new elements in. New text or a new strong inside a paragraph is a change to that paragraph, and the paragraph is the right size for its mark. The trouble is content that went away or moved: there is nothing left to put a mark on, so the mark goes on the parent, whole.

Every shape of change, and where it shows

The change Wears the mark today Proposed
Words in a paragraph The paragraph. Right size. The same.
A new element with an ID It, as new. Right size. The same.
A new table row The row. Right size. The same.
An element removed Its parent, whole. Too big. A mark where it was.
Elements in a new order Their parent, whole. Too big. The ones that moved.
A new element inside a parent marked for a removal or a move Nothing: the parent's mark covers it. Its own mark. Flipping shows it first, then the parent.
Loose text right inside a big container, with no paragraph around it The container. The same. Our pages put text in paragraphs; if this shows up, the formatter can wrap it.
A top-level section removed or moved Nothing: body wears no mark. Covered by the removal and move marks.

Recommendations

  1. Give tr an ID, as hr and svg elements have: a unit, not a scope you pick. A new row wears one mark. A click between cells still picks the table, and a directive whose cell is gone lands on the table. Rows keep their IDs through a markdown edit of the table. Built.
  2. A parent's own content leaves out which children it holds and their order. It keeps its tag, attributes, and the text and inline elements right in it. Removals and moves get marks of their own (3 and 4), so the parent wears a mark only for a change to itself. It goes in with 3, since alone it would leave a removal with no mark anywhere.
  3. Show a removal where it was. Drawn above as a green line in the gap, with its own check, placed by the parent and the sibling before it that is still there. A line between siblings doesn't read right everywhere: not in an svg, a sentence, or a nested section. Another way: show the original in place, grayed out. The research recommends a tombstone drawn from the element's old box on screen.
  4. A move marks what moved. The fewest children that moved, from the order you saw and the order now; its tip says where it was. Not needed, so build it only if it's cheap. One shape: a removed spot where it was, which the move points to.
  5. A new element covers what's inside it, and nothing else does. Everything inside something new is new, so one mark is right there. Inside a changed element, a new one wears its own mark, and flipping goes inner first. The yak “Notices: a scope changed only by a removal or reorder covers the new elements inside it” holds it.

Order: 5 is small and changes only the domain, so it goes before the first release of notices. 2 and 3 go together. 4 waits.

Research

  • HTML editing by command: the report. The field splits two questions: what can be named (identity) and what may go where (the HTML content model). A scope answers a third: what you pick. Our replace, add, remove, and move are the field's update, insert, delete, and move, and addressing by an ID kept in the file is the choice that survives edits by others. Its recommendations: say “unit” for what the command addresses and keep “scope” for what you pick; phrasing elements and text never addressable; and maybe an edit <id> that swaps one string for another inside a unit. Its questions for you.
  • Showing changes to an HTML document: the report, with drawings. Tools put a removal in one of three places: in place, struck through or faded; as a marker where it was; or in the margin. A move is a linked pair, from and to, wherever it's handled. A gap line doesn't generalize: it fails in an svg. What does generalize is the element's old box on screen, which the tab can measure just before the swap takes it out. Its recommendation: a tombstone in the overlay, a line in the gap or a dashed outline of the old box, with its own check and the first words of what went; a click on it shows the original in place, grayed out, for one look; a move is a tombstone at the old spot paired with a mark at the new one, one notice, one ack. It rejects keeping ghosts in the page until you ack: the page would stop matching the file. Its options drawn, and its questions for you.

Planned: marked, not shown

Step 5 of the plan. Nothing moves under you. A changed element wears its mark and keeps showing what you last saw until you click show. Once shown, the change offers ack and view previous, and later reject. Ack takes the mark off. A reload shows the newest of everything, each change still marked and waiting for its ack.

When you edit content the agent changed and you haven't shown, yours wins, and the agent's change is passed over. The green crop marks go when you start changing the content, and come back if you undo your way to where you started; then you can still show the agent's change. A directive there is no conflict.

Magenta words are your actions.
